# Locked decisions and retired conclusions

## Locked

Device type is not a randomised treatment. It is a block or a classification only. Treatment conclusions stay with the eight tested devices.

The response is charging rate as displayed SOC gained divided by elapsed minutes, not elapsed minutes alone.

The 40% to 60% SOC window is used for every session. Screen wakes follow one schedule across devices and treatments.

The cable threshold is 60 W from the 20 V by 3 A USB-C PD cap, not 27 W.

Windows laptops are excluded because they cannot take all four treatments. The device list must form complete blocks.

Draw class is set by effective draw under the two standard USB-C PD chargers used in the study, against the 60 W cable-cap threshold. Published specification is the starting point. Effective 23 Nov 2026. D1, D2 and D8 are high; D3 to D7 are low. The Honor Power (D7) is rated 66 W only over private SCP with the original charger, and its measured ceiling on the study chargers is about 38 W, so it is low despite a published value above 60 W.

The named model stays additive. A three-way term is not added to the model; the device-power comparison is handled by planned contrasts.

## Retired

Inferring draw class from elapsed time inside the 40% to 60% window. Retired on 23 Nov 2026. Do not restore it. The response time is an outcome of charging, not a power meter. A direct measured ceiling on the study chargers is a different thing and is used for the Honor Power only.

Device type as a treatment factor. Do not restore it.

Elapsed minutes as the response. Do not restore it.

The 27 W cable threshold. Do not restore it.

The claim that Group by Charger by Cable uses 3 df. That interaction has 1 df.

The claim that the 8 to 12 hour station time was absent from the proposal. It is in the submitted text without a recorded derivation.

Treating a MacBook Air M1 as a high-draw device on the basis of nameplate power.
